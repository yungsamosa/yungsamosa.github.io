Bank customer churn model

Predicting which bank customers will leave, using a model simple enough to explain to the people who would act on it.

Role
Solo
Timeframe
December 2024
Built with
Python, pandas, statsmodels, scikit-learn
Code

Overview

Banks lose money every time a customer closes an account, and keeping a customer is cheaper than finding a new one. The useful question is not only who will leave, but what about them predicts it, because that is what a retention team can act on.

The data came from a Kaggle Playground competition: 165,034 customers with credit score, age, tenure, balance, products held, activity status, country, and whether they left. The task was to score the probability of churn for another 110,023.

Build

I used logistic regression rather than a black-box model, so every coefficient reads as a direct statement about customers. I started with a plain binomial GLM in statsmodels to see which variables carried signal, then moved to an L1-penalized model in scikit-learn.

  • Tested an age-by-balance interaction on the theory that older customers with large balances behave differently. It was not significant (p = 0.07), so I dropped it.
  • One-hot encoded country and gender, and left estimated salary, balance, and credit score out of the final model.
  • Checked the training fit against five-fold cross-validation to make sure the score was not an artifact of fitting the whole training set.

Outcome

The model reached an AUC of 0.818 on the training data and 0.817 under cross-validation, so it generalizes about as well as it fits. I submitted churn probabilities for all 110,023 test customers.

  • Inactive members and customers holding fewer products were far more likely to leave. Each additional year of age raised the odds of churn by roughly ten percent.
  • German customers churned noticeably more than French or Spanish ones, and women more than men.
  • Next time I would benchmark against a gradient-boosted model to measure how much accuracy the interpretable model gives up, and engineer features such as a zero-balance flag.
